Motivation

DarkLight looks for a new light boson in e⁺e⁻ pairs

  • A light vector boson coupled weakly to charge — a dark photon A′ — is a minimal extension of the Standard Model. Anomalies in nuclear transitions (the "X17") point to a mass near 17 MeV.
  • DarkLight at TRIUMF: 30 MeV electrons from the ARIEL e-linac on a thin tantalum target. An A′ radiated by a beam electron decays to e⁺e⁻.
  • Two magnetic spectrometers measure the e⁻ and the e⁺: a narrow peak in the pair mass above the QED background.

m²ee ≈ 2 p₊ p₋ (1 − cos θ₊₋)

  • A 1 % error in the momentum scale moves a 17 MeV peak by 0.17 MeV — more than the 0.125 MeV mass resolution the sensitivity studies assume.
  • So the momentum scale must be known well below 1 %, from data.
Calibrating the DarkLight spectrometers · DarkLight · October 20262 / 29
The collaboration's sensitivity code (MainzGen) uses a 30 MeV beam and a 0.125 MeV signal resolution. Both momenta scale together if the beam energy or a common field constant is off; then m scales by the same factor. This talk uses the 2025–26 commissioning runs with a carbon target.

The detector

Two spectrometers, two GEM planes each, a Hall probe in every magnet

Schematic, not to scale. In each arm a dipole bends the electrons upward through a 0.25 mm aluminium exit window onto two GEM tracking planes (0.4 mm strips); scintillators above them trigger the readout. On the bottom GEM the dispersive coordinate X measures the momentum — 4–7 mm per 1 %. A Hall probe in each dipole logs the field throughout every run.

Right arm at 20° (positrons in production, electrons for calibration), left arm at 36° (electrons). The dipole current selects the central momentum. The beam polarity of an arm is set by the current sign.

The method

Calibration with processes we can calculate

  • Elastic e–¹²C: the electron keeps the beam momentum to 5×10⁻⁴ (carbon recoil). One sharp line on the GEM; its position measures pbeam / pspectrometer.
  • Møller e⁻e⁻ → e⁻e⁻: the momentum falls steeply with angle, so the band on the GEM is tilted. It measures beam energy, field and angle in a different mix.
  • In most Møller runs one arm sits on the elastic line and the other on the Møller band, recorded in the same events.
  • Data: carbon target, beam 9.8–28.7 MeV, dipoles from 11 to 200 A.

Elastic: p′ = E / (1 + E/M (1 − cos θ)). Møller: E₃ = m (E₁ + m + (E₁ − m) cos²θ) / (E₁ + m − (E₁ − m) cos²θ). At 28.6 MeV the left arm at 165 A sees the elastic line at 36°, the right arm at 37 A the Møller band at 20°.

The method

A simulation that follows every run

  • Every simulated event takes the dipole fields from that run's Hall probes at that moment: p₀ = K × |Bprobe|, one constant K per arm and probe position.
  • The beam energy comes from a per-run table measured with the elastic lines — not from the magnet setting (slide 11).
  • Simulated GEM signals go through the same clustering and fits as the data.

What works

The magnets, not the analysis: field maps explain 97 % of the offset

  • The magnets were mapped at TRIUMF before installation: 3.35 % above the calculation at 200 A. The field per amp rises 0.84 % from 200 A down to 150 A (in-situ Hall probes).
  • Together 4.2 % predicted against 4.4 % needed (right arm, 150 A): residual 0.13 %, within the 1 % current calibration of the mapping.
  • Open: the left arm needs about 0.6 % more field than the right at 200 A.

An early claim of a 6.3 % lab/calculation ratio compared a 200 A map with a calculation normalised at 192 A; at equal current the maps give 2.1–3.4 %, depending on the power-supply calibration. A single scale describes the 200 A field to 0.1 %; the field per amp falls 0.5 % over 135–170 A, exactly the trend the beam data show.

What works

The beam energy is set by the accelerator RF, not the bending magnet

  • All elastic lines, both arms, fitted together with one beam momentum per accelerator tune.
  • Tunes differ from the bending-magnet (MB4) value by up to 3.6 %, and both spectrometers agree.
  • Same magnet current, different RF phases: −1.4 % (runs 4090 → 4091, 35 min apart).
  • A per-run beam table now feeds every analysis (cooker and Python).
  • The absolute scale rests on the reference tune's magnet value: ±0.9 %.

What works

A hidden foil: the simulation skipped the exit window

The 0.25 mm aluminium exit window sits about 23 mm below the bottom GEM — inside the vacuum volume that held the GEMs and triggers. Geant4 tracks a particle in only one of two overlapping volumes, so no simulated electron ever crossed the foil. Fixed by building that volume from its contents. Lines centred on their own fitted positions, normalised to the peak.

What works

¹²C excited states are too weak to calibrate with

An inelastic line a fixed energy below the elastic one would fix the absolute momentum scale on its own. Left arm, 41 M events: predicted 0.1 and 0.2 counts of the 4.44 MeV state, 90 % upper limits 444 and 441. At these momentum transfers (q ≈ 0.08 fm⁻¹) the state is 10⁻⁶ of the elastic rate.

What works

Toward cross sections: carbon in the event generator

  • The elastic generator weighted events with the cross section of a charge-1 nucleus with a proton form factor — fine for line shapes (carbon reweighting moves a line by 0.3 µm), wrong for counting.
  • New carbon option: Z = 6 in every term, the measured ¹²C charge form factor, the McKinley–Feshbach Coulomb factor. Off by default: proton, tantalum, aluminium outputs bit-identical.
Calibrating the DarkLight spectrometers · DarkLight · October 202617 / 29
Form factor: Fourier–Bessel fit of de Vries, de Jager and de Vries, ADNDT 36 (1987) 495 (r_rms 2.46 fm). Coulomb: McKinley and Feshbach, Phys. Rev. 74 (1948) 1759. Radiative terms scale as in Maximon and Tjon, PRC 62 (2000) 054320. Checks: Z = 6 over default = 36.002; McKinley–Feshbach factor 1.02038 in the generator vs 1.02035 analytic at 20°.

What works

Both arms counted in the same events

  • One arm on the elastic line, the other on the Møller band: their ratio cancels beam current and DAQ dead time.
  • The GEMs keep ~400 ns of signals, so each arm also records electrons near the other arm's trigger — independent of its own trigger.
  • 28.6 MeV set (29 runs): elastic 159k in own-arm events, 126k in other-arm events; Møller 0.52M and 110k.
  • A cooker plugin does the counting and reproduces the band analysis exactly.

Open: the Møller bands sit ~10 mm from the simulation

  • With each run's measured beam energy: right arm +10.2 mm (rms 2.5, 42 runs), left −12.0 mm (rms 5.3, 5 runs).
  • Not enough alone: a constant field offset (excluded, 7σ), the other magnet's stray field (0.2 mm), survey corrections (≤ 2.5 mm), the exit foil.

Open questions

The elastic lines' Y offset, and how it carries over to the Møller band

  • What it is: Y is the non-dispersive coordinate, the image of the exit window. Sliding the simulated Y distribution until its shape matches the data gives the offset: the data sit 6.0 mm lower (right arm, median of 36 lines) and 9.4 mm lower (left, 71); per accelerator tune it ranges from −9.5 to +2.3 mm.
  • An elastic line is vertical on the GEM: the shift leaves its X, the momentum calibration, untouched.
  • Applied to Møller: every simulated Møller event is moved down by the offset of its tune (the arm's median where the tune has no elastic line: 34 of 42 right-arm runs), and all cuts are redone. The tilted band moves by ~1.8 × the shift in X.
  • Result: right arm +10.2 → −0.9 mm, left −12.0 → −0.1 mm (open symbols, slide 19).

Open questions

Open: the band's own Y image wants a smaller shift — and is wider

  • The Møller band's own Y image prefers a smaller shift: −2 mm in this run, −3 mm over all right-arm runs, against −6 mm from the elastic lines. With it, about half of the right-arm band offset remains.
  • Edge by edge the data image is wider: lower edge −3.0 mm, upper +1.0 mm (medians of 28 and 22 runs). No single Y offset can do that.
  • Survey corrections, a vertical beam offset or a 0.5 G field each move the image by 3 mm or less.
  • Candidates: the beam position on the target (slide 22), optics, apertures, the field at low current.

Open questions

Why the beam position moves the Y image but not its width

  • A pinhole camera: the chamber is an aperture between the target and the GEMs. Its size sets the width of the image; where the beam spot sits sets where the image lands. Moving the spot moves the image the other way, with the same width.
  • Simulation (run 4405, only the beam moved): -2.5 mm of image per mm of beam position, width unchanged.
  • Data, right arm at 160–180 A: between accelerator tunes 89 and 93 the simulations agree (+6.2 and +6.5 mm), but the data image moves by 7.5 mm — about 3.0 mm of beam position. Nothing else changed.
  • So the usual −6 mm may be where the beam usually sits, not a detector offset.
Calibrating the DarkLight spectrometers · DarkLight · October 202622 / 29
Tune 89 (runs 4170–4173) and tune 93 (4186, 4187, 4192): same magnets, GEMs and simulation; the accelerator RF was fully retuned between them (injector phase −29° → −130°, beam energy 3.6 % lower in tune 89). The exit window, 10–23 mm below the bottom GEM, would pin the image edges if it limited the acceptance; in the simulation the image moves, so an aperture further upstream sets the Y acceptance. The dipole in between scales the shift (×2.5 on the right arm) but does not change the principle. Caveat: a beam offset moves the right-arm Møller band by −1.2 to −2.3 mm per mm in u, the opposite of what the Y-shift correction of slide 20 does, so the beam position alone does not explain the Møller offsets.

Open questions

Open: Hall probes remember the magnet's history

  • Right arm at 28 A, other magnet off: the probe reads 500.0 G after ramping up from zero, 506.0 G after coming down (+1.2 %).
  • The Møller bands of two such runs (3692, 3776) coincide to 0.06 mm: the field did not change, the probe reading did.
  • A simulation driven by the probe then misplaces the band: this explains the 6.6 mm between those runs — but not the offset common to all right-arm runs. Other run pairs are mixed.

Open questions

Open: what triggers the detector between beam pulses?

Trigger times folded with the accelerator's 909 µs cycle (13.6 µs beam pulses at 1100 Hz). With the trigger gated on the pulse (run 4250) every trigger falls at one phase (concentration 0.999). With the gate off (run 4180) the triggers spread over the whole cycle (concentration 0.03): only 1.8 % stand above the flat level near the pulse. So the trigger scalers count mostly non-beam pulses, the two arms' recorded fractions disagree, and the absolute luminosity is on hold.
Calibrating the DarkLight spectrometers · DarkLight · October 202624 / 29
Fold with the period fitted to each run's first 20k triggers; the TDC clock drifts by tens of µs over that span, which smears the gate to ~20 µs. Per-arm recorded fractions from the scalers: f_A = 1.93×10⁻³, f_B = 2.53×10⁻³ (run 4250). The Møller/elastic ratio needs neither.

Summary

Summary

Established

  • A simulation that follows every run: field per event from the Hall probes, beam energy per tune, the run's GEM readout.
  • Elastic lines agree with data to 0.03–0.05 % over 55–170 A; the 4 % field offset is the magnets.
  • The beam energy follows the accelerator RF; a per-run beam table feeds all analyses.
  • A missing exit window found and fixed: the beam spread is 0.26–0.55 %.
  • Carbon cross section in the generator; both arms counted in the same events.

Open

  • Møller band offsets (+10 / −12 mm) and widths; Y images that are lower and wider.
  • Hall-probe hysteresis at low field.
  • Triggers between beam pulses; absolute luminosity.
  • Absolute beam energy (±0.9 %).
  • Next: refit everything with the foil geometry (running), then the Møller/elastic ratio → the Møller cross section.
